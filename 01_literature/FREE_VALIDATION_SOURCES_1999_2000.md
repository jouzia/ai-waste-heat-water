# Free validation-source recovery: Martínez-Díez lineage

Date: 2026-09-30

## Purpose

Document legally discoverable, no-cost sources that can support the membrane-distillation validation track without purchasing the 1999 article.

## 1999 primary paper

Martínez-Díez, L. & Vázquez-González, M.I. (1999), *Temperature and concentration polarization in membrane distillation of aqueous salt solutions*, Journal of Membrane Science 156(2), 265–273. DOI: 10.1016/S0376-7388(98)00349-4.

The article record confirms the primary experiment and its subject matter. The numerical observation ledger remains blocked until primary-source figures/tables or a traceable author/institutional copy is obtained.

## 2000 same-author follow-up

Martínez-Díez, L. & Vázquez-González, M.I. (2000), *A method to evaluate coefficients affecting flux in membrane distillation*, Journal of Membrane Science 173(2), 225–234. DOI: 10.1016/S0376-7388(00)00362-8.

The accessible ScienceDirect text gives the experimental design for pure-water DCMD: different bulk temperatures with a bulk temperature difference of about 10 C and a recirculation rate of 7 cm3/s. It also explicitly describes the mass-transfer coefficient, membrane heat-transfer coefficient, boundary-layer heat-transfer coefficient, and temperature-polarization analysis.

This is not a substitute for the 1999 primary observations. It is a source-matched methodological cross-check from the same research lineage and can help reconstruct the model equations and parameter-identification logic.

## 2000 polarization follow-up

Martínez-Díez, L., Florido-Díaz, F.J. & Vázquez-González, M.I. (2000), *Study of Polarization Phenomena in Membrane Distillation of Aqueous Salt Solutions*, Separation Science and Technology 35(10), 1485–1501. DOI: 10.1081/SS-100100237.

A publicly indexed copy exposes detailed nomenclature and experimental discussion, including the decomposition of flux into ideal, temperature-polarization, vapor-pressure/concentration, and concentration-polarization contributions. This source is useful for equation cross-checking and interpretation, but numerical observations are not promoted to primary 1999 data.

## Institutional provenance

The University of Málaga research portal lists the authors' 1999 and 2000 publications, providing an institutional provenance trail for the research lineage.

## Scientific policy

- No values are copied into the 1999 observation ledger solely because a later paper reproduces them.
- Secondary sources are used for corroboration and equation cross-checking only.
- Primary-source validation requires source locators and, for figure-derived values, archived pixel coordinates, axis calibration, repeat extraction and digitization uncertainty.
- The 1999 benchmark remains quantitatively blocked until this gate is satisfied.
- Independent open-access experimental datasets remain eligible for the main unfitted validation track.

## Current implication

The free-source search has materially improved the validation pathway: the project now has an accessible same-author methodological paper and an institutional provenance trail, while retaining the strict distinction between primary observations and secondary evidence.
